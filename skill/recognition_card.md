# Recognition card for film readers: technique numbers + the COUNT rule

Copied from Coach's manual (`Offensive Manual 2027/chapters/B9_defensive_recognition.md`, ID-1 and ID-2). Regenerate this file from B9 when B9 changes; B9 wins if they differ.

Note for readers: "our linemen" below means the men on the offense's line, counted out from the snapper; on opponent film the offense is the other team.

## ID-1. Technique numbers and gaps

**What it is.** A technique number tells where a defensive lineman lines up on our linemen. We count from the center out. The numbers are the same on each side.


| Tech | Where he lines up |
|---|---|
| 0 | Head-up on the center. |
| 1 | On the center's shoulder, in the A gap. |
| 2i | On the guard's inside shoulder. |
| 2 | Head-up on the guard. |
| 3 | On the guard's outside shoulder. |
| 4i | On the tackle's inside shoulder. |
| 4 | Head-up on the tackle. |
| 5 | On the tackle's outside shoulder. |
| 6 | Head-up on the tight end (Y) or the wing. |
| 7 | On the Y's inside shoulder. |
| 9 | On the Y's outside shoulder. A "wide 9" is well outside. |

We use the old-school numbers on the Y: 6 = head-up, 7 = inside, 9 = outside. Some manuals call these 6 / 6i / 7. We do not.

| Gap | Where it is |
|---|---|
| A | Between the center and the guard. |
| B | Between the guard and the tackle. |
| C | Between the tackle and the Y. With no Y, it is outside the tackle. |
| D | Outside the Y. |

**Rules for the number.**
- Count from the center out, one man at a time. The man can be any player on the line. In an unbalanced set, a receiver with an eligible number can be covered up, and 3 or 4 men can be on one side. The 1st man out from the center gets 2i / 2 / 3. The 2nd man out gets 4i / 4 / 5. The 3rd man out gets 7 / 6 / 9.
- Find the ball first. The center is the snapper. He is not always in the middle of the line.
- If a defender is on the center's helmet, he is a 0. This is true when he leans or when you cannot see his helmet.
- An end on a side WITH a Y: name the exact spot by his width. 5 = tackle's outside shoulder, 7 = Y's inside shoulder, 6 = head-up on the Y, 9 = outside the Y.
- An end on a side with NO Y: tight on the tackle's outside shoulder = 5; a wide 5 = 9. This one is a judgment (the gaps are not uniform), so 5 or 9 are both accepted there.
- If you are not sure between a 0, a 1 and a 2i, look again. 0 = on the center's helmet. 1 = on his shoulder. 2i = on the guard's inside eye.

> **Cue:** "Helmet = 0"



## ID-2. Counting the front: the COUNT rule

**What it is.** We name the front by the number of defenders on the line at defensive line depth. The nose does not decide the front. The nose only tells the spacing.

**The COUNT rule.**
1. Find the defenders on the line at defensive line depth.
2. Count each one with a hand down.
3. Count each standing end on the line at end depth.
4. If the count is 4, the front is EVEN.
5. If the count is 3 or 5, the front is ODD.
6. Then note the man on the center: 0, 1, or nobody.

**Who counts as a lineman.**
- A player who stands on the line at end depth and plays like a lineman is a defensive end. He counts.
- If three hands are down and a standing end is on the line, the front is a 4-man EVEN front.
- A player who stands off the ball at linebacker depth is a linebacker. He does not count.
- An overhang who is walked up, or split out over a receiver, is not an end. He does not count. This is the 3-4 outside linebacker.
- With two hands down and two wide standing ends that play like linemen, the front is still EVEN.

**The nose describes the spacing.** If 4 are on the line and one is a 0, the front is EVEN with odd spacing. If 3 are on the line and the nose is a 1, the front is still ODD.

**Against two Ys or two wings.** Count from the outside of one Y to the outside of the other Y. Count each defender on the line over or outside the Ys and wings. Do not stop at the tackles.

**Check the Y before you call ODD.** Some defenses stand a 7 or a 9 on the Y. On film he blends into the Y. If a defender is within about 1.5 yards of the Y at end depth, he is on the line.

**Count the line two times.** A tight 3 beside the guard is easy to miss. If you miss him, you call the wrong front. You also count the box short by one.

**Two exceptions (Coach).**
- If the center and both guards are covered, the front is BEAR. This is true with only 4 on the line.
- If a 0 nose plays with a linebacker walked up on the line, the front is ODD. This is true when 4 end up on the line.

> **Cue:** "Count, then the nose"

**Scout evidence.**
- OPA (2026): 3 hands down plus a standing end that played like a lineman = EVEN on almost every snap.
- OPE (2026): 3 down in a 4i-0-4i with two walked-up outside linebackers = ODD on all 48 of Coach's tags. Film readers who counted the linebackers called EVEN on 27 snaps.
- OPJ (2026): readers called 5-0-5 on snaps that were 5-0-3-5. The 3 sat tight on the guard.
- OPQ (2025 film): wide standing edges on the line count as linemen. With a Y they were 7s and 9s; with no Y, 9s.

**Ends (Coach's chart 11/01, and Coach 11/09). The chart page is `Offensive Manual 2027/chapters/B9a_techniques_gaps_chart.md`. With a Y on that side the order from the tackle out is 5, 7, 6, 9: name the exact one, don't skip 7 / 6. With NO Y on that side there is nothing to measure him against and the gaps are not uniform ("it's a little grey"): tight on the tackle = 5, "a wide 5 with no tight end is a 9". Two readers who write 5 and 9 for the same no-Y end agree. That split is not a miss and does not go on the check list.**
